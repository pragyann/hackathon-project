# MentorME Futura Remix Hackathon - Challenge Brief

> Extracted from `source-pdfs/Hackathon - Topic Challenge.pdf` (MentorME Academic, 4 Sep 2026, 9 slides). This is the authoritative statement of what the organisers are asking for. Content that only existed as slide graphics has been transcribed by hand.

**Theme:** Build for Australia - AI Solutions for Industry and Communities

## 1. Hackathon objectives

Australia is entering a period of significant workforce transformation as digital technologies and AI reshape how organisations operate. While recent Australian Government research suggests Generative AI is currently augmenting rather than replacing jobs, employers continue to face persistent workforce shortages, with the national vacancy fill rate remaining at only 68.2% in March 2026. These trends highlight growing demand for graduates and professionals who can combine technical capability with critical thinking and practical problem-solving.

Australia's higher education sector plays a critical role in developing this future workforce. Between January and May 2026, 680,582 international students studied in Australia, while Higher Education enrolments continued to grow despite broader declines across other education sectors. As student cohorts become increasingly diverse, universities are placing greater emphasis on career readiness, industry engagement and preparing graduates for an AI-enabled workforce.

At the same time, AI is rapidly moving from experimentation to enterprise adoption. Per KPMG's Global Tech Report 2026, 53% of organisations report they still lack the talent needed to deliver digital transformation successfully, and 92% believe managing AI agents will become an important workforce capability within five years. Future professionals will need more than technical knowledge: they must be able to apply AI responsibly to solve real-world challenges.

## 3. Challenge tracks

### Track 1 - Future of Work and International Talent

**Challenge question:** How might we use AI, data and digital technology to help international students and skilled migrants access meaningful career opportunities, while helping Australian employers identify, develop and retain diverse talent?

**Your challenge:** Design an AI powered solution that improves employment outcomes for international talent while helping Australian organisations better identify, develop and retain skilled people. The solution should demonstrate clear value for users, be technically feasible, and address a genuine workforce challenge in Australia.

**Context:** Between 2022 and 2024 international student numbers grew ~38% to more than 850,000 nationally (617,674 in 2022; 783,862 in 2023; 850,849 in 2024; 844,368 in 2025; 680,582 Jan-May 2026). This growth contributes significantly to Australia's economy and workforce, and it increases the need for more effective pathways that help international talent transition successfully into employment.

**The problem:**

- *International talent face barriers:* understanding local recruitment processes; translating overseas qualifications; **building professional networks**; gaining relevant Australian experience.
- *Employers face challenges:* identifying transferable skills; evaluating international experience; connecting with suitable candidates; retaining diverse talent.
- These issues create a disconnect between available talent and labour market demand.

**Challenge areas:** Career Navigation | Skills Recognition & Job Matching | Employability & Career Readiness | Employer Recruitment & Workforce Planning

**Example solution directions:** AI Career Advisor (personalised guidance on career pathways, roles and next steps) | Skills Translation Platform (translate overseas skills and experience into Australian standards) | AI Employability Coach (feedback on applications, interviews and job readiness) | Employer Matching Platform (match talent with employers on skills, potential and culture fit) | Workforce Analytics Dashboard (real-time insights on talent pipelines, skills gaps and demand trends) | AI Onboarding Assistant (smoother onboarding and integration for international hires)

### Track 2 - Education and Student Success  ← **our track**

**Challenge question:** How might we use AI, data and digital technology to improve student learning, engagement, academic support and preparation for employment?

**Your challenge:** Design an AI powered solution that enhances student learning, engagement and academic success while strengthening career preparation and employability. The solution should demonstrate clear value for users, be technically feasible, and address a genuine workforce challenge in Australia.

**Context:** Total student enrolments increased from ~1.42 million in 2015 to 1.67 million in 2024, driven by growth in both domestic and international student numbers. Over the same period overseas enrolments increased by more than 60%. As cohorts become larger and more diverse, universities face growing pressure to deliver personalised learning experiences, timely academic support and effective career preparation **at scale**. These changing expectations create opportunities for AI powered solutions that improve student engagement, learning outcomes and employability throughout the education journey.

The slide also flags the **Top 5 challenges of using AI in education**: biased information & AI hallucinations; ethics & transparency; data collection & systemisation; public policy; basic technological infrastructure.

**The problem:**

- *Students face barriers:* accessing the right support at the right stage of their journey; support services operating across separate systems and teams; lack of connected, personalised experiences; changing needs not identified early.
- *Universities face challenges:* pressure to improve engagement, learning outcomes and employability; operating under financial and resource constraints; more than 40% of universities in deficit over the past five years; funding per domestic student down 6% since 2017.
- Common challenge: "how to identify students who need support earlier, deliver more personalised learning experiences and better prepare graduates for successful careers without proportionally increasing resources."

**Challenge areas:** Personalised Learning Pathways | Student Engagement & Support | Academic Success & Wellbeing | Career Preparation & Employability

**Example solution directions:** AI Student-Support Assistant | Personalised Learning Recommendation Platform | Student Engagement & Early-Support Dashboard | Employability & Skills Tracking Tool | Mentoring Management System | Portfolio & Career Readiness Platform

## 4. Challenge guidelines

**Expectations.** A team does not need to solve every part of the challenge. Identify **one clear problem, one main target user and a realistic solution** buildable within the hackathon. Strong submissions demonstrate:

- A clearly defined user problem.
- Evidence that the problem is important.
- A focused and realistic solution.
- A working prototype or convincing proof of concept.
- A clear explanation of how the technology works.
- Consideration of implementation, privacy, fairness and accessibility.
- A measurable benefit for users, organisations or communities.

**What teams can build:** an AI agent or assistant; a web or mobile application; a data analytics dashboard; a recommendation or matching system; an automation tool; a predictive analytics solution; a digital service or platform; a working prototype supported by a clear implementation plan.

Teams are encouraged to use AI, data and digital tools **where they add genuine value**. Using AI for the sake of including AI will not score higher. Teams should clearly explain why the selected technology is appropriate for the problem.

**Key constraints:**

- *Responsible use of AI and data:* privacy and data protection; bias and fairness; transparency; security; appropriate human oversight; responsible use of personal and employment information.
- *Accessibility and inclusion:* usable by people with different cultural and language backgrounds, levels of digital confidence, education and professional experience, and accessibility requirements.
- *Practical implementation:* realistic for an Australian employer, university, training provider or community organisation to implement.

**Teams should avoid relying on:** data that would not realistically be available; unreasonable budgets; technology that cannot be explained; fully automated high-impact decisions without human review.

## 5. Team structure

4 to 5 participants, roles split clearly to reflect the vibe-coding nature of the event.

| Role | Number | Main responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software Engineer / Vibe Coder | 1-2 | Build the prototype using AI coding tools, manage technical implementation, test core features, support the demo |
| Business Analyst | 1 | Define the user problem, map requirements, clarify the workflow, make sure the solution solves the right problem |
| Data Analyst / Data Scientist | 1 | Support data use, AI logic, analytics, evaluation, metrics, dashboarding, model-related decisions |
| Business / Communication Lead | 1 | Shape the value proposition, prepare the pitch, explain impact, communicate to judges |

Titles need not match exactly, but every team must cover: product and problem understanding; technical build; data, AI or analytics thinking; business value and communication; final pitch and demonstration.

## 6. Submission requirements

1. **Problem statement** - what problem, who experiences it, why it matters.
2. **Proposed solution** - what it does, who uses it, how it improves the current situation.
3. **Prototype or proof of concept** - working application, interactive prototype, dashboard, technical demonstration or validated product concept.
4. **Technology overview** - tools and technologies used, how the main components work together, what data or AI models are required, important technical limitations.
5. **Implementation plan** - how it could be introduced, which organisation or partner could use it, resources required, key risks and next steps.
6. **Impact measurement** - how success would be measured (e.g. increased employment outcomes, improved job or skills matching, increased student engagement, reduced time to access support, better employee retention, improved user satisfaction).
7. **Final pitch and submission** - present problem, solution, prototype and implementation approach to the judging panel.

**Only two artefacts are actually submitted:** pitch slides (PDF or PPTX) covering the requirements above, and a demo showcasing the prototype or proof of concept.

## 7. Pitch format and judging criteria

**First round:** 5 minutes for pitch and demonstration, then 5-10 minutes of judges' questions. Judges may stop presentations that exceed the allocated time.

**Recommended pitch structure:** 1. The problem. 2. The target user. 3. The proposed solution. 4. The prototype demonstration. 5. The technology. 6. The expected impact. 7. Implementation and next steps.

**Scoring, out of 100 points:**

| Criterion | Weight | What it focuses on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and User Understanding | 15% | Clearly defines a specific problem and primary target user; understands real user needs and pain points; shows why the problem matters and its relevance to the track; demonstrates understanding of the Australian context |
| Solution Quality and Innovation | 15% | Directly addresses the identified problem; clear and meaningful value proposition; creativity or meaningful improvement over existing approaches; uses AI/data/digital tech in a relevant and purposeful way |
| **Prototype and Technical Execution** | **35%** | Functional and convincing prototype demonstrating the core solution; effective use of AI/vibe-coding tools; understanding and ownership of the technical approach and AI-generated outputs; explains how components, technologies and data work together; demonstrates testing and awareness of technical limitations and risks |
| Evaluation, Impact and Future Development | 20% | Explains the business, education or community value; meaningful and measurable success indicators; critically evaluates limitations, risks and trade-offs; considers privacy, fairness, security, accessibility and human oversight; realistic implementation and next steps with potential to scale |
| Pitch and Live Demonstration | 15% | Communicates problem, solution and value clearly; smooth and convincing demo of the actual prototype/workflow; explains key technology and implementation decisions; presents within time; responds confidently to questions |

## 8. What this means for us

- **35% of the score is the working prototype.** Scope must be cut to whatever can be demoed live end to end.
- **50% (Problem + Evaluation/Impact) is non-code work.** The PRD, metrics, risk and privacy sections carry as much weight as the build.
- Judges explicitly penalise unavailable data, unexplainable technology and fully automated high-impact decisions. Every data source we depend on needs a legitimate, demonstrable answer, and every AI recommendation needs a visible rationale and a human in the loop.
