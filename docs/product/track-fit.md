# Track fit: does our idea match Track 2?

**Verdict: yes, Track 2 is the correct track, and the fit is unusually tight.** Our idea also touches Track 1, which is worth understanding precisely so we can use Track 1's evidence in the pitch without ever drifting into it.

## Track 2's four challenge areas vs our two pillars

The organisers list exactly four challenge areas for Track 2. Our idea lands squarely on two of them and touches a third:

| Track 2 challenge area | Our coverage |
| --- | --- |
| **Personalised Learning Pathways** | **Direct hit.** Pillar 1 is literally a personalised learning pathway, generated from the student's own coursework and target role. This is the core of the product. |
| **Career Preparation & Employability** | **Direct hit.** Both pillars serve this: the roadmap prepares them, the networking gets them in front of employers. |
| **Student Engagement & Support** | **Genuine fit, and stronger than it first appears.** A product designed to be used every semester from first year to graduation *is* a sustained engagement mechanic. We are not an at-risk early-warning system, but "engagement" in Track 2's sense is about staying connected to students across the journey, which is exactly what the semester loop does. |
| Academic Success & Wellbeing | Not addressed, and we should not pretend otherwise. |

The brief says "Participants may choose to focus on **one or more** of the following areas", so covering two well is exactly what is being asked, and better than spreading thin across four.

## Against Track 2's example solution directions

The organisers name six example directions for Track 2. Ours is essentially **"Personalised Learning Recommendation Platform"** fused with **"Employability & Skills Tracking Tool"** and a slice of **"Portfolio & Career Readiness Platform"**. We are not inventing something outside their frame; we are combining three of their own examples along a single student journey, which is a defensible answer to the "creativity or meaningful improvement over existing approaches" line in the rubric: the novelty is the join, not the parts.

## Against Track 2's stated problem

The brief's Track 2 problem statement lists what students face:

- *"Accessing the right support at the right stage of their journey"* - **the closest match in the entire brief to what we are building.** Note the exact wording: not "the right support", but "the right support **at the right stage of their journey**". A stage-aware roadmap that gives a first-year direction and a final-year triage is a literal answer to that sentence. This should be quoted directly in the pitch.
- *"Support services operate across separate systems and teams"* / *"lack of connected, personalised experiences"* - our thesis almost verbatim. Job boards, roadmap sites, career services and event platforms are four disconnected systems, and we are the connective tissue.
- *"Changing needs not identified early"* - partial fit, and better than it looks. We do not detect academic risk, but the whole premise of engaging students from first semester is that career needs get surfaced years earlier than the final-year career appointment that currently surfaces them. What we identify early is the employability gap, not the academic one.

And what universities face: pressure to improve employability under financial constraint, with 40%+ in deficit and funding per domestic student down 6% since 2017. Our implementation story (personalised career guidance for a whole cohort at the cost of serving the few who book appointments) is a direct answer to that pressure.

The summarising line on the Track 2 slide is: *"how to identify students who need support earlier, deliver more personalised learning experiences and better prepare graduates for successful careers without proportionally increasing resources."*

Read against a product that starts in first semester, we now answer **all four** clauses, which was not true of the final-year-only framing:

- *"identify students who need support earlier"* - we engage from first semester, years before career services typically reach a student. We should be precise that we surface career-readiness gaps early, not academic risk.
- *"deliver more personalised learning experiences"* - the roadmap is derived from the individual's own transcript.
- *"better prepare graduates for successful careers"* - both pillars.
- *"without proportionally increasing resources"* - the cost of serving a student for six semesters is roughly the cost of serving them for one, which is the argument to a university sector with 40%+ of institutions in deficit.

## Why the networking pillar does not push us into Track 1

Worth being careful here, because it is the one thing a judge might question.

"Building professional networks" appears explicitly as a barrier under **Track 1's** problem statement, not Track 2's. But Track 2's own challenge question ends with "**and preparation for employment**", and its challenge areas include "Career Preparation & Employability", so employment preparation is unambiguously in Track 2's remit. Networking is a means of career preparation, and we are serving students preparing for work, not migrants already in the labour market.

The line that separates the tracks is the **user and the employer side**:

- **Track 1** is two-sided. Its challenge is to improve outcomes for international talent *while helping Australian employers identify, develop and retain* people. Half its example directions are employer-facing: Employer Matching Platform, Workforce Analytics Dashboard, AI Onboarding Assistant.
- **Track 2** is student-side, inside the education journey.

We are student-side and single-sided. We never build the employer product. **If we start building an employer-facing matching or analytics feature, we have drifted into Track 1** and should either commit to that or drop the feature. This is the concrete tripwire to watch during the build.

## Using Track 1's evidence without switching tracks

We serve all students, and international students are a large sub-segment of them, so Track 1's evidence is legitimately available to us as *context about part of our user base*. It is also stronger evidence for the networking argument than Track 2's own slide provides. We can cite: the 680,582 international students in Jan-May 2026, the ~38% growth to 850,000+ between 2022 and 2024, and Track 1's explicit finding that international talent struggle with *building professional networks* and *understanding local recruitment processes*.

Use it carefully. The claim to make is *"the organisers' own research identifies network-building as a barrier, and it is most acute for the international students who make up a large share of Australian cohorts"*, not *"we are building for international students"*, which would invite the question of why we are not in Track 1. Cited this way it earns the "demonstrates understanding of the Australian context" line under Problem and User Understanding without muddying our track.

## One risk to manage

Two pillars across a whole degree is more surface than a single-purpose entry, and the rubric rewards *"one clear problem, one main target user and a realistic solution"*. Serving every year level rather than only final-years widens it further.

The mitigation is framing, and it holds up: this is **one** problem (the gap between what a degree gives you and what getting hired requires, discovered far too late) with two halves, for **one** user, not two products stapled together and not a platform for everybody. Lead with the problem and the personas, never with market size.

If time pressure forces a cut during the build, cut depth in pillar 2 rather than dropping it. The roadmap-plus-networking loop is what makes us more than the "Personalised Learning Recommendation Platform" the organisers already listed as an obvious answer, and the stage-awareness is what makes us more than a static roadmap site. Those two things are the differentiation; everything else is negotiable.
